The transformation begins with an unformed block of dense blue jeweler’s wax. The artisan sculpts the wax cylinder using hardened hand files and chisels, establishing an organic finger band geometry. The finished wax master is encased in a gypsum investment flask and baked until the wax completely melts and vaporizes without ash. Molten 925 sterling silver is cast into the empty mold chamber, solidifying into solid metal. Finally, the raw cast silver ring undergoes sprue removal, internal radius smoothing, and multi-stage rouge polishing on cotton wheels to achieve mirror reflectivity.

The Principle

You shape the form.
Metal remembers every gesture.

Lost-wax casting (cire perdue) is an ancient metallurgical discipline where an original sculptured pattern of wax is entirely replaced by molten precious metal.

The process begins in blue carving wax — a dense, highly stable compound formulated to hold razor-sharp file cuts, crisp planes, and tactile undulating ridges without softening under body warmth. Every nuance carved into the wax master transfers directly to the finished casting.

01 / Density

High-Density Carving Wax

Formulated specifically for fine bench chiseling. Holds micro-bevels and clean ridges without cracking.

02 / Mold

Gypsum Investment

Liquid refractory plaster hardens around the carved pattern, capturing every microscopic ridge in reverse.

03 / Metal

Solid 925 Silver

92.5% elemental silver alloyed with 7.5% copper for structural durability, tactile heft, and mirror shine.

The Foundry

The wax is lost.
The silver endures.

During the burnout cycle, the investment mold is heated until the carved wax pattern melts and vaporizes cleanly away, leaving a hollow negative chamber.

Molten 925 sterling silver is poured into the void under vacuum or centrifugal pressure. As the liquid metal cools, it solidifies into an exact duplicate of the hand-carved original — capturing every bevel and curve in solid silver.

Stage 02 · Foundry Cast

Molten sterling silver displaces the vaporized master.

Bench Refinement

From raw casting
to mirror clarity.

Once divested from the gypsum mold, the raw silver ring reveals the sculpted form with a matte satin casting texture.

The jeweler saws the casting sprue flush, smooths the interior core for ergonomic daily wear, and advances through progressive abrasive grits. Rotary cotton wheels with jeweler’s rouge impart deep specular reflectivity across the undulating silver facets.

Stage 03 · Sterling Silver

Hand-buffed on cotton wheels to mirror specular luster.

Frequently Asked Questions

Key technical aspects of lost-wax casting and hand wax sculpture.

Jeweler’s carving wax is a dense, specially formulated carving compound. Unlike soft candle waxes, it remains rigid at room temperature, allowing precise scraping, saw cutting, file shaping, and detailing without melting or deforming under hand warmth.

The name refers to the destruction of the original wax sculpture during production. The carved wax is encased in heat-resistant plaster investment and baked until the wax melts and vaporizes completely away, leaving a void that is filled by molten precious metal.

Sterling silver is an alloy composed of 92.5% pure elemental silver and 7.5% copper. Pure silver alone is too malleable for daily jewelry wear; the addition of copper grants structural rigidity, tensile strength, and wear resistance while preserving brilliant silver reflectivity.

Yes. Because molten silver enters the cavity as liquid under pressure, it conforms to the microscopic contours of the mold walls. Every chisel groove, rasp tooth mark, and organic facet created in the wax pattern remains permanently cast in the solid metal.
